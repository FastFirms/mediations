Parenting Plan Template

Discussion worksheet for separated parents in Australia · Mediations Australia · Version 1.0, 8 November 2026 · mediationsaustralia.com.au/parenting-plan-template/

Important – please read first. This worksheet helps parents discuss and write down practical arrangements for their children. It is general information, not legal advice, and it is not a court document. A parenting plan is not a court order and is not enforceable in the same way. If you already have parenting orders, get legal advice before agreeing to anything different. If there is family violence, abuse or a risk to anyone's safety, do not use this worksheet to negotiate directly: call 000 in an emergency or 1800RESPECT on 1800 737 732, and get professional help.

How to use this worksheet

  • Work through each section and write down what you agree. Leave a section blank if it does not apply.
  • Be specific: days, times, places and who is responsible.
  • Focus on your children's needs, ages and routines. There is no automatic starting point of equal time.
  • A parenting plan must be in writing, dated and signed by both parents (Family Relationships Online). It can include ways to change arrangements and resolve disagreements, and it can be renegotiated over time.
  • If you cannot agree, a family dispute resolution practitioner can help. Sessions do not have to be in the same room; telephone, video or separate-room arrangements may be available.

Parenting plan or consent orders?

Parenting planA written, dated agreement signed by both parents. Flexible and can be renegotiated. Not a court order.
Consent ordersOrders made by the Court on agreed terms, after it considers the child's best interests. Enforceable as court orders. Get legal advice before applying.
