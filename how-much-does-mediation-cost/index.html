Family & Separation Mediation

Family mediation covers parenting arrangements, property settlement, consent orders, and the full range of post-separation decisions. It is the area where government-subsidised alternatives also exist — making it the most cost-variable type of mediation in Australia.

Government-funded Family Relationships Online (Family Relationship Centres) offer free or low-cost FDR for parenting matters — useful for simpler arrangements, though private mediation handles complexity and combined property/parenting matters that FRCs typically can't. Our Access Mediation pathway offers assessed reduced fees from $495 + GST per person for eligible participants.

Workplace & Employment Mediation

Workplace mediation resolves conflicts between employees and employers, between colleagues, and in cases involving bullying, harassment, unfair dismissal, or breakdown in a working relationship. It is most often initiated by the employer — but employees can engage Mediations Australia independently.

The Fair Work Commission provides a free conciliation service for unfair dismissal and general protections claims — useful, but limited in scope and outcome flexibility. Private workplace mediation gives employers and employees more control over timing, outcome, and confidentiality.

Commercial & Business Mediation

Commercial mediation covers contract disputes, partnership disagreements, supplier or customer conflicts, franchise disputes, and business relationship breakdowns. Fees reflect the commercial complexity involved and are typically higher than family matters — but still a fraction of commercial litigation costs.

Commercial litigation — even a relatively simple contract dispute — routinely costs $50,000–$200,000+ per side before it reaches a hearing. Federal Circuit and Family Court of Australia data shows the median commercial dispute running 18–24 months. Mediation resolves the same matter in a day, with a binding outcome both parties craft.

Estate & Wills Disputes

Estate disputes — contesting a will, challenging the distribution of an estate, or resolving executor conflicts — are emotionally charged and, in court, can consume a significant portion of the estate itself in legal fees. Mediation is particularly effective here because the parties are usually family members who will remain connected long after the dispute ends.

Contested probate proceedings and family provision claims under the Succession Act can run to six figures in legal costs — often more than the parties would have received had they simply mediated. The Attorney-General's Dept — Family Dispute Resolution notes that FDR is effective across a wide range of civil disputes including estate matters.

Property & Real Estate Disputes

Property disputes span strata and neighbour conflicts, construction disagreements, real estate transaction disputes, and boundary or easement issues. These matters often involve people who will continue to have an ongoing relationship — making a negotiated resolution far more durable than a court order.

Many property disputes can also be brought before VCAT or state tribunals at low cost — but tribunal proceedings are formal, adversarial, and create a public record. Private mediation resolves the same matter confidentially, usually in a single session.

How Mediation Compares to Court — Across All Dispute Types

Regardless of dispute type, the same fundamental comparison holds. Courts are expensive, slow, adversarial, and public. Mediation is fast, private, and leaves the outcome in your hands. The figures differ by matter, but the direction never does.

Court cost ranges are indicative and vary significantly by complexity and jurisdiction. See FCFCOA fee schedule for current Family Court filing fees as a benchmark for family matters.

Why Mediation Is Worth It — for Any Dispute

Across every dispute type, mediation delivers the same core advantage over litigation: you stay in control of the outcome. A court imposes a decision; mediation produces an agreement. Agreements reached through mediation have a far higher voluntary compliance rate than court orders, because both parties had a hand in making them.

The other constants: mediation is confidential (no public record), typically resolved in days rather than years, and preserves whatever ongoing relationship exists — whether that's a co-parenting arrangement, a business partnership, or a neighbour you'll see every day.
