Family Mediation

How long does family mediation take?

In short: Most family mediation sessions are completed in a single day — typically four to six hours. Complex matters involving both parenting and property may require a second session, but most disputes are resolved in one appointment.

Typical session length

A standard family mediation day runs from around 9 am to 3 or 4 pm, with breaks. The first hour is usually spent on introductions and ground rules; the remaining time is focused negotiation. Most families reach a workable agreement before the end of the day.

What affects the time?

FactorTypical impact
Parenting arrangements onlyHalf day to full day
Property settlement onlyFull day
Parenting and property combinedOne to two full days
High conflict or complex assetsMay require a second session
Shuttle mediation (separate rooms)Often slightly longer than joint sessions

How does this compare to court?

A contested parenting or property matter in the Federal Circuit and Family Court of Australia typically takes one to three years from filing to final hearing, with multiple interim appearances along the way. Mediation resolves most matters in a single day — and the agreement reached is binding once formalised.

Preparation speeds things up

Parties who arrive with a clear sense of their priorities, a completed financial statement (for property matters), and relevant documents typically reach agreement faster. See our mediation preparation tool to get ready before your session.
