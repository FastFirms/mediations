Key takeaway: Mediation typically costs $2,500–$6,000 in total — shared between both parties. Contested court proceedings for the same dispute typically cost each party $30,000–$150,000. For the vast majority of separated Australians, mediation is not just cheaper — it is exponentially cheaper, faster, and far less damaging to the people involved.

What mediation actually costs

The cost of mediation in Australia depends on whether you use a private mediator, a community-based organisation, or a government-funded service. Here is a realistic breakdown:

What affects the cost of mediation

Several factors influence where your mediation falls within the typical price range:

  • Complexity of the issues. A simple property settlement with one jointly-owned home resolves more quickly than one involving a business, investment properties, SMSFs, and complex debt structures. More complexity means more session time and higher cost.
  • Number of issues. Matters involving both unresolved property and parenting disputes may require separate sessions or a longer combined session.
  • Degree of conflict between the parties. High-conflict situations — where communication has broken down or trust is severely damaged — often require shuttle mediation and more careful management. This adds time and cost.
  • Whether parties come prepared. Parties who arrive with financial documents organised and priorities clear reach agreement faster. Unprepared parties take longer and ultimately pay more.
  • Experience of the mediator. More experienced mediators, particularly those with specialist legal knowledge, typically charge higher rates but often resolve matters more efficiently.
  • Online vs in-person. Online mediation eliminates venue costs and travel, often reducing the total cost — and is equally effective for most matters.

What going to court actually costs

Any honest conversation about mediation costs must compare them to the alternative. Contested family law court proceedings typically cost each party:

Stage of proceedingsTypical cost (per party)
Filing and initial preparation$5,000–$15,000
Interim hearings (1–3 days)$10,000–$30,000
Disclosure, subpoenas, interlocutory steps$5,000–$20,000
Final trial (3–5 days)$25,000–$80,000
Expert reports (valuer, accountant, psychologist)$5,000–$30,000
Total (per party, contested trial)$50,000–$175,000

Court fees (FCFCOA filing fees) are separate — and they add up: an initiating application costs $365, interim hearings cost $545/day, and a first trial day costs $1,090. A three-day trial generates $2,725 in court fees alone, on top of all the legal costs above.

A contested matter also routinely takes 12–24 months from filing to final hearing. During that period, both parties pay ongoing legal fees for correspondence, interlocutory applications, and preparation — costs that accumulate even while the case sits waiting in the court queue.

The last row is critical. Approximately 95% of family law matters that enter the court system ultimately settle without a final trial — often through negotiation or mediation on the steps of the courthouse. Most people who pursue litigation end up at a negotiated outcome anyway — just $50,000–$100,000 later than if they had mediated from the start.

That money comes out of the asset pool. A couple with $600,000 in assets that incurs $100,000 in combined legal fees distributes $500,000 — reduced by one sixth before it is even divided between them.

Low-cost and free mediation options

For people who cannot afford private mediation fees, there are government-supported alternatives:

  • Family Relationship Centres (FRCs): Funded by the Australian Government, FRCs provide free Family Dispute Resolution for parenting matters (up to one hour free, then an income-assessed fee). Contact the Family Relationship Advice Line on 1800 050 321
  • Legal Aid commissions: Each state and territory has a Legal Aid commission providing free or low-cost legal advice and, in some cases, funded mediation. Eligibility is income-tested.
  • Relationships Australia: Provides FDR on a sliding-scale fee basis across most states and territories — 1300 364 277
  • Community Legal Centres: Free legal advice and assistance for people on low incomes, particularly for early-stage issues

What if I genuinely can't afford mediation?

If cost is a genuine barrier, practical options include: asking the other party to contribute more if there is a significant income disparity; using a government-funded service first (accepting longer wait times); considering online mediation to eliminate travel and venue costs; or starting with a limited-scope session focused on the most urgent issues rather than attempting to resolve everything at once.

Getting the most from your mediation investment

Mediation is most cost-effective when both parties arrive prepared. Gather all financial documents at least 2–3 weeks before the session. Get independent legal advice so you understand your rights and entitlements. Clarify your priorities — what matters most, what you can flex on, and what your bottom line is. Agree to share valuations rather than each commissioning your own expert. And come ready to listen, not just to argue your position. These steps reduce session time and improve outcomes.

Frequently asked questions

Who pays for mediation — me or my ex?

Mediation costs are typically shared equally between both parties. Some mediators, including Mediations Australia, invoice each party separately for their share. If there is a significant income disparity, it is reasonable to negotiate a different split. In some cases, one party covers the full cost to initiate the process quickly — knowing the cost will be factored into the overall settlement.

Is mediation tax deductible?

Mediation costs related to a property settlement may be partially tax deductible if the dispute involves income-producing assets such as an investment property or business. Mediation costs for parenting matters are generally not deductible. Seek advice from your accountant for your specific situation.

Is online mediation cheaper than in-person?

Often yes — online mediation eliminates venue costs, reduces travel costs for both parties, and can sometimes be completed more efficiently. Our online sessions are equally effective for most matters and available Australia-wide regardless of where you are located.

Can I get a refund if mediation doesn't reach agreement?

This depends on the mediator's fee structure. At Mediations Australia, we use fixed fees agreed upfront. Where a session cannot proceed due to circumstances beyond the parties' control, we have a fair cancellation and rescheduling policy that we explain at the time of booking.
