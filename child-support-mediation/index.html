Child support: the basics

Child support in Australia is administered by Services Australia — Child Support, using a formula that takes both parents' taxable incomes, the cost of children at different ages, and the percentage of care each parent provides. For most families the standard assessment works fine — a number gets calculated, payments flow, and life moves on.

Where it doesn't work fine is where mediation comes in: disputes about the assessment, special expenses the formula doesn't capture, complex or shared care, or where parents want a private arrangement better tailored to their situation.

When mediation helps

  • Disputes about income — particularly with self-employed parents
  • Special expenses — private school, medical, extracurriculars — above the standard formula
  • Care percentage disputes — which directly affect the payment
  • Significant change in circumstances — job loss, new family, relocation
  • Building a private agreement instead of going through Services Australia
  • Where child support and parenting arrangements are entangled — better resolved together

The formula — and where it falls short

The standard formula is administrative and broad-brush. It captures:

  • Both parents' taxable incomes (with a self-support amount set aside)
  • The cost of children, based on research-derived figures
  • The percentage of care each parent provides
  • The number and ages of children

What it doesn't capture well: special expenses, unusual care patterns, the realities of self-employment, contributions in kind, and the parties' own preferences. That's why private agreements exist. See the full child support guide →

How the mediation process works

  1. Initial consultation — understanding the dispute or what you want to agree.
  2. Financial disclosure — income, expenses, special costs for the children.
  3. Pre-mediation intake — separately with each parent.
  4. The session — working through the assessment, special expenses, and any care arrangements.
  5. Agreement — either a Child Support Agreement (registered) or a private arrangement.
  6. Implementation — registered with Services Australia if agreed, or self-managed.

Private vs agency-collected

Parents have three main options for how child support runs:

  • Self-management — you agree, you pay, no agency involvement.
  • Private collection — Services Australia assesses but doesn't collect.
  • Agency collection — Services Australia assesses and collects.

Mediation often produces self-managed or private arrangements where the relationship supports it.

Many parents prefer a private agreement over agency involvement. Mediation helps you build one that lasts.

Special expenses

The formula covers ordinary costs of raising children. Above-formula expenses — private school, orthodontics, expensive extracurriculars, special medical needs — are exactly where parents most often disagree. Mediation lets you agree:

  • Which expenses count as special
  • How they're shared (50/50, in proportion to income, otherwise)
  • How they're paid — directly to providers, reimbursed, or pooled
  • How decisions about new expenses get made

Child support and parenting

Care percentages feed the formula, which means disputes about parenting time can become disputes about money — and vice versa. Mediating them together produces a coherent outcome where both pieces fit. See child custody mediation →

Questions & answers

Child Support Mediation — frequently asked

Do I have to use the standard child support formula?+

No. The formula is the default, but parents can agree their own arrangement — either a Child Support Agreement registered with Services Australia, or a private self-managed arrangement.

How are special expenses (school, medical) handled?+

The standard formula doesn't cover them well. Mediation lets you agree which expenses count, how they're shared, and how new ones get decided.

Can child support be mediated alongside parenting arrangements?+

Yes — and it often should be, since care percentages affect the formula. Mediating both together produces a coherent outcome.

What if my ex's income isn't what they're declaring?+

Income disputes — especially with self-employed parents — are a common driver of child support mediation. The process lets you work through documents, capacity, and what a fair assessment looks like.

Can I have a child support agreement without Services Australia?+

Yes — you can self-manage entirely, or register the agreement with Services Australia for them to enforce. Mediation produces agreements that work either way.

How long does child support mediation take?+

Most matters resolve in a single session, with the whole process from consultation to documented agreement typically completing in 2–4 weeks.

Is the mediated agreement legally binding?+

If registered as a Child Support Agreement with Services Australia, yes. Private agreements bind by contract but may need formalisation for full enforceability.
